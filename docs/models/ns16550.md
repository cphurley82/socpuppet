# UART (16550)

`sp.Ns16550()` · C++ `socpuppet::Ns16550` · registry name `ns16550`

## What it stands for

The serial port that a firmware's console prints through. 🎓 A UART sends bytes one bit at a time down a wire. The 16550 is the design nearly every PC and a great many SoCs copied, so almost every operating system has a driver for it, and Zephyr's is called `ns16550`.

## What it does

```python
bus = platform.add("bus", sp.Router())
uart = platform.add("uart", sp.Ns16550())
bus.map(uart.socket, base=0x1000_0000)

platform.build()
platform.run(sp.us(10))
print(uart.output)      # what the firmware has printed so far
uart.write("spam\n")    # typed at the other end of the serial line
```

🧵 Whoever holds the UART is the other end of its serial line: `output` is what came down the wire, and `write` is the keyboard.

- **Transmits.** A byte written to the transmit register is "sent": it is added to `uart.output`.
- **Is always ready.** The line status says the transmitter is free straight after every byte, so a driver that polls it never waits.
- **Receives.** What is typed with `uart.write` waits in the UART, and firmware reads it from the receive register a byte at a time, in order. The line status says "data ready" until the last byte has been read.
- **Interrupts, if its line is connected.** `uart.irq` goes to an interrupt controller's input, or straight to a CPU. It is high while an interrupt that firmware enabled is pending, and the interrupt identification register says which. A console that is polled leaves the line unconnected.
- **Has the 16550's registers.** Eight of them, one byte each: transmit and receive, interrupt enable, interrupt identification and FIFO control, line control, modem control, line status, modem status and scratch. Opening the divisor latch turns the first two into the baud-rate divisor, as on the chip.

### The two interrupts

| Interrupt | Enabled by | Pending | Ends when |
|---|---|---|---|
| A received byte is waiting | bit 0 of the interrupt enable register | while the line status says "data ready" | the last byte is read |
| The transmit register has emptied | bit 1 | from when a byte is sent, or the interrupt goes from disabled to enabled | firmware reads the interrupt identification register and is told of it |

When both are pending the identification register says the received byte, which has the higher priority, and the other is still there afterwards.

💡 **The transmit interrupt is for the register becoming empty, and not for its being empty.** Being told once is the whole of it, until the next byte has gone. That is the chip's behaviour, and it is how a driver starts to send: it enables the interrupt, and is interrupted at once because there is room.

## What it leaves out

- **A receive FIFO with a size.** Everything typed waits, however much it is, so nothing is ever overrun. The FIFO control register is remembered and ignored, and the identification register says there is no FIFO, so a driver sends a byte for each interrupt.
- **The other interrupts.** A 16550 can also interrupt for a line error and for a change on a modem line, and the model has neither to report.
- **A debugger's look at a computed register.** A debug read gets the word the borrowed model has stored. Line control, modem control and the scratch register are stored, so they read true. The receive buffer, interrupt enable, interrupt identification and line status are computed when read, so a debugger sees a stale value there. A debug write is declined (see [upstream.md](../upstream.md)).
- **Time.** A byte is sent the instant it is written. The baud rate you set is remembered and ignored.
- **The wire.** No start and stop bits, no parity, no framing errors, no modem lines.

## In a devicetree

`compatible = "ns16550"`, with `reg-shift = <0>`, since its registers are a byte apart, and the clock a real one divides down. Its node is `uart@...` in Zephyr's dialect and `serial@...` in Linux's, and Linux's `chosen` names it as the console by its path, `stdout-path = "/soc/serial@10000000"`. 💡 It is a 16550 and not a 16550A, the one with a FIFO, because that is what its registers say. OpenSBI's 8250 driver and Linux's take either name.

## Where it comes from

💡 This one is borrowed. The model is the PULPino UART from [VPV-Peripherals](https://github.com/VP-Vibes/VPV-Peripherals), a library of SystemC peripheral models built on SCC. It was written at the Technical University of Munich for the PULPino microcontroller, whose UART is a 16550 with its registers four bytes apart.

socpuppet wraps it in an adapter (`src/socpuppet/models/ns16550.cpp`) that does three things:

- puts the registers one byte apart, where the original 16550 has them and where a driver expects them by default;
- catches each transmitted byte, which the model sends out of a TLM socket, and keeps it as the output;
- keeps the bytes that have come in, hands the model each one when it asks, and tells it whether another waits;
- gives a read of the empty receive register a value, which the model leaves unset.

The model's interrupt is patched (`cmake/patches/vpv-peripherals-uart-interrupt-from-state.patch`): as borrowed it lost track of what was pending. What was wrong and what the patch does is in [upstream.md](../upstream.md).

What socpuppet relies on is written down as tests, in `tests/cpp/contracts/uart_contract.h`. Any UART that passes them can take this one's place. The model's own source is short and worth reading: [`pulpino/uart.h`](https://github.com/VP-Vibes/VPV-Peripherals/blob/main/pulpino/uart.h).
